How to Identify a Phishing URL

Phishing relies on tricking people, not just machines. These tips help you spot dangerous links before you click or type anything.

🔒
Check for HTTPS

A padlock means traffic is encrypted, but HTTPS alone does not prove a site is trustworthy — phishing sites use it too.

🌐
Look at the real domain

Read the domain right before the first single slash. Extra subdomains or an "@" can disguise the true host.

🔍
Watch for typosquatting

Look-alikes like paypa1.com or g00gle.com swap letters for numbers to imitate a brand.

📏
Beware very long URLs

Long links can hide the true destination or stop you reading carefully.

🖥
IP-based URLs

Legitimate services rarely send you to a raw IP like http://192.0.2.10/login.

🔑
Suspicious keywords

"verify", "secure", "account", "update" packed into a URL are common in credential-harvesting pages.

⚠
Urgency & social engineering

"Your account will close in 24 hours" creates panic so you act before you think.

🚫
Never enter credentials into suspicious links

When in doubt, type the known address yourself instead of clicking.

Password hygiene & MFA

  • Use long, unique passwords for every site
  • Never reuse your email password elsewhere
  • Turn on multi-factor authentication (MFA) where available
  • Use a reputable password manager

Reporting suspicious links

  • Do not forward the link to others
  • Report it to your IT/security team or email provider
  • Delete the message once reported
  • If you entered credentials, change them immediately and enable MFA
